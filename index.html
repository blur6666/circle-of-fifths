Working it

Mask arrows slide the window over a wheel that stays put; Wheel arrows turn the wheel under a window that stays put, the way a cardboard one does. The layer you last moved starts pulsing — the window outline breathes, or the rim and hub glow. One press is one slice, either direction, for as long as you like. Reset puts C back on top and keeps the current movement mode armed.

The centre always reads out the key the window is on.

The window is a key

Three slices wide over the major and minor rings, one slice over the diminished ring — and that is not a decorative shape. It is exactly the seven chords built on a major scale, and no others. With C in the middle:

major ringFCGIV · I · V
minor ringDmAmEmii · vi · iii
diminishedB°vii°

So the window is a chord palette. Move it one slice and every chord in it changes to the next key's, which is why writing that wanders round the circle sounds like it is going somewhere rather than jumping.

The rings

Major — the key. Clockwise is up a fifth and one more sharp; anticlockwise is down a fifth and one more flat.

Relative minor — the minor key sharing that signature. A minor and C major are the same seven notes started in different places.

Diminished — the triad on the seventh degree, a leading-tone chord that commonly resolves towards the tonic.

Key signature — a real engraved staff rather than a count, so you read the sharps and flats where they actually sit. Every one is drawn at the same size, which is why the outer edge is ragged: each hangs out as far as its own width needs.

The one slice with two names

Six sharps and six flats are the same pitches under different names, so that slice carries both — Gb/F# — and its staff shows both signatures. The rest of the wheel commits to one spelling each.
